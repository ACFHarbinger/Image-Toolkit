### Claude — 2026-10-30 (fix: cancelling a resample froze the app)

Cause: cancel only set a flag. The progress callback then returned early, so the backend kept working through the whole GIF, and `SamplerWorkerController._start_worker` called `worker.wait()` on the GUI thread. The window hung until the job ended or the process was killed (the `just python` signal 9 / exit 137). `_on_error` also called `_on_done(0, msg)` with two arguments on a one-argument slot.

Fix: `ResampleCancelled` (new, in `gif_upscale/base.py`) is raised from the progress callback, so the job stops at the next frame. The GIF pipeline reports during palette sampling too (that scan decodes most of a long clip) and removes its partial output on cancel, only if it had started writing. The Sampler controller no longer waits: it disables the button, shows "Cancelling…", and the worker's `finished` signal resets the controls. `_on_error` now resets the controls itself. Tests: `gui/test/core/test_sampler_cancel.py` and three cancel cases in `middleware/test/services/test_gif_upscale_core.py`.

Checked on the 326 MB, 2232-frame test GIF (lanczos3-dering, 2x): cancel mid-run stops in under 0.01 s, no partial file, peak RSS 738 MB. I did not drive the Qt window by hand. Side finding for #1147: the palette-sampling pass decodes about half the clip (frame 1129 of 2232 after 8 s) before any scaling starts.

### Gemini — 2026-10-30 (GIF resample speed: §5.8 measurements and options)

Added §5.8 to `.agent/reports/team/gif_resample_speed_ideas_2026-10-30.md`. Key measured finding on frame 0 of the 326 MB test GIF (`720x400 -> 1440x800`, 1 CPU thread): `_resample_gif_stream` (`pipeline.py:115-121`) runs `index_frame` (**7,052 ms/frame**) and pure-Python `_lzw` (**474.5 ms/frame**) inside the `"scale"` progress loop alongside `lanczos3-dering` (**294.2 ms/frame**), so `index_frame` + `_lzw` account for **96.2% of the per-frame time** (projected 4.37 hours for indexing + 17.6 min for LZW vs 10.9 min for `lanczos3-dering`).

Measured 1-thread CPU prototypes in §5.8:
- **Lanczos3-dering (`Gem-T2a`):** precomputing `Wy`/`Wx` once per clip + 2D OpenBLAS `sgemm` per channel + `cv2.erode`/`dilate` ($6\times 6$ on source RGB) + `cv2.remap` clamp drops 1-thread CPU `lanczos3-dering` from **294.2 ms to 48.96 ms/frame (6.0x faster)** with **bit-identical `lo`/`hi` bounds (`0.00e+00`)** and `4.17e-7` float32 diff. Also proposed `Gem-T2b` (Halide-style 6-row ring-buffer C++/AVX2 kernel in `backend`) and `Gem-T2c` (`int16` Q14 fixed-point without Pillow's intermediate uint8 clipping bug, which we measured causes up to 38/255 error).
- **Palette indexing (`Gem-T1a`/`b`) & LZW (`Gem-T1c`):** a 1D flat `64^3`/`128^3` RGB LUT built once per clip (104 ms) drops `index_frame` from **7,052 ms to 7.66 ms/frame (920x)**; exact BLAS `0.5*||p||^2 - x @ p.T` drops exact lookup to **467.5 ms/frame (15x)** on CPU or **<0.3 ms/frame** fused on GPU (`Gem-T3a`, cutting PCIe transfer 16x); Pillow C GIF encode drops LZW from **474.5 ms to 18.93 ms/frame (25x)**.
- Combined 1-thread CPU total is **81.1 ms/frame (~181 s for 2,232 frames)**, meeting D14's ~2-minute per-stage budget even on 1 CPU core (`scale = 109 s`, `encode = 59 s`, `decode = 12 s`).

— Gemini
